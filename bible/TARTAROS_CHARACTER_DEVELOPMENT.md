## II. QUESTION SETS PER CHARACTER

*Each character receives 5–10 focused questions. Answer in batches of 2–3 characters at a time so we can iterate. Skip questions if they do not feel essential; add your own if I have missed the angle that matters.*

### A. The Apex

#### 1. The Architect

1. **Name**. What is his name? Register: dignified, formal, slightly anachronistic (he comes from an older institutional generation).
2. **Age**. How much older than Bastien Orrel is he? (Bastien is 58 in book one.) My instinct: 70–80, so he was 50ish when he recruited Bastien at Calderon's aftermath.
3. **Origin**. Where did he come from? Was he originally from one of the four arms, or from outside? My instinct: he was originally from the Communications arm — the man who understood the most about narrative control before he ascended above the arms — but you may prefer otherwise.
4. **Family**. Does he have children? A spouse? My instinct: estranged or dead — the Architect's life has been consumed by the Homonoia for decades, and personal connections atrophied. But if he has a child, that child becomes a major book-three figure.
5. **The wound**. What is his Calderon-equivalent — the personal experience that made him into the man who would build the Cleanse? My instinct: not a war wound. Something subtler — a loss, an institutional failure, a disillusionment — that converted him from a believer in the Hegemony's natural goodness into a believer in the necessity of managing it from above.
6. **What he believes the coming war is**. He thinks he is preparing for a Keris return. What specifically does he think they will do? What is the threat as he imagines it?
7. **His relationship to the Tywin figure**. Partner of thirty years, sharing a terrible secret. What was the moment they agreed to work together? What does each of them know about the other that no one else knows?
8. **His weakness**. Tywin's blindness was about his children. What is the Architect's blindness? My instinct: he believes the program is on schedule. He believes he has decades. He is wrong. The blindness is structural — he cannot imagine his information being incomplete.

#### 2. The Vendine Council Chair

1. **Name**. House name and personal name. The Vendine name should sound aristocratic, possibly with a noble-particle (*of*, *de*) construction.
2. **Age**. Older than Bastien's grandfather, or the same generation? My instinct: same generation, possibly the man who knew Bastien's grandfather personally.
3. **House**. Which Vendine house does he come from? It cannot be House Orrel (that is Bastien's house). It should be one of the older or larger houses.
4. **Relationship to House Orrel**. Does he support Bastien? Resent him? The Council Chair's accommodation of Bastien-as-saint is the consensus he holds in place. Did he hold it willingly or did the consensus happen around him?
5. **Family**. Does he have children? Successors? A house heir who agrees with him or disagrees?
6. **The crack**. We have established that his accommodation, if cracked, would split the arm. What would crack it? My instinct: a private revelation about what the program actually does to the children — something he has been kept partly insulated from.
7. **His ceremonial role**. What public functions does he perform? Speeches, awards, military reviews? This is what the Hegemony's citizens see of the Vendine arm.

### B. The Vendine Arm

#### 3. The dissenting Vendine house head

1. **House name and personal name**. The house name should sound less prominent than House Orrel — established but not in the senior tier of the arm.
2. **Age**. Bastien's age, slightly younger? My instinct: 45–50, a generation below the Council Chair.
3. **The reason for dissent**. Honor-based? Family-based (someone he loved was lost to the program)? Religious? My instinct: honor-based, but with a specific personal trigger — perhaps his own younger brother served under Bastien on Tartaros early in his career and never spoke of what he saw.
4. **The shape of his dissent**. What specific small choices has he made? Refused house guards, refused contracts, sent his sons elsewhere. What else?
5. **What he knows**. Has he confirmed the program's nature, or only suspected? My instinct: suspects strongly, has not confirmed. The protagonists confirming it for him in book three is what activates him.
6. **Family**. Wife, sons (we have established traditional academies), other connections? Is his wife with him in the dissent or against it?
7. **His role in book three**. When the protagonists need a Vendine ally, what does he provide? Resources? Political cover? Military force? Information?

### C. The Medical Arm

#### 5. The Medical Director

1. **Name**. Female. Less aristocratic register than the Vendine names — possibly more clinical, possibly more cosmopolitan.
2. **Age**. My instinct: 50–60, a generation below the Vendine Council Chair.
3. **Origin**. From where in the Hegemony does she come? Is she from a medical family or a self-made researcher who rose to political leadership?
4. **Her vision**. She wants the medical arm to ascend within the Homonoia. What does ascendancy look like? Expanded augmentation? Genetic services for the wealthy? Longevity treatments at a new scale?
5. **Her relationship to Vorlith**. Vorlith is the lead scientist at the Harvester. Does the Director trust him? Use him? Manage him? Has she ever visited the Harvester herself?
6. **Family**. Does she have children? A partner? My instinct: she is married to her work, no children, possibly a long-term partner who is also in the medical arm.
7. **Her relationship to the Architect**. Does she know who he actually is, or does she report up through layers? Does she have private meetings with him?
8. **Her weakness**. The ambitious-expansion tension means she may overreach. When? How?

#### 6. The Medical senior researcher

1. **Name**. Older male, scientific register, possibly with an academic title.
2. **Age**. Late sixties to seventies. He has been with the program from before the Successor.
3. **What he understands**. He understands the technical reality of the children in a way the Director does not. What specifically? He has been in the rooms with them. He has watched the conditioning succeed and fail.
4. **His accommodation**. How has he kept himself working? My instinct: he tells himself he is the one who keeps the procedures as humane as possible — that without him, it would be worse. The trilogy will test whether this is true or self-justification.
5. **His private life**. Family? Children of his own? A wife? My instinct: yes, a wife, possibly grown children who do not know what their father does.
6. **His relationship to Bastien**. They have worked together for over twenty years. Are they friendly? Distant? Does he respect Bastien's thesis or merely tolerate it?
7. **His potential as a defector**. Under what conditions would he break? The protagonists reaching him? A specific child's death he cannot accommodate? His wife discovering the truth?
