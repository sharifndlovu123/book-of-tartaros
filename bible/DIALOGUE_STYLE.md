# The Tartaros Cycle — Dialogue & Character-Voice Style

*How dialogue is written in this trilogy. The goal is not "realistic talk" — it is
**round, distinct characters**. Read this before drafting or revising any scene
with people in it. It anchors on the Game-of-Thrones model (class- and
culture-marked speech, subtext, wit as armor, POV-colored narration), grafts one
lesson from Harry Potter (every character gets a single unforgettable signature),
and keeps this trilogy's own rule: **register by diction, never by phonetic
spelling.** Companion to `TARTAROS_CYCLE_BIBLE.md` (characters), `CLAUDE.md`
(prose voice), and `../reference/CHARACTER_MAP.md`.*

---

## The one rule

**Dialogue exists to make characters round and distinct — not to move information.**
A line that only delivers a fact is a failed line; make the fact arrive *through*
a voice that could belong to no one else.

> **The cover-the-tags test.** Delete every "X said." If you can still tell who is
> speaking, the dialogue is working. If two characters could swap lines without
> anyone noticing, both voices are wrong.

Flat dialogue is almost always *interchangeable* dialogue. Roundedness is the cure,
and it is built, not wished.

---

## The three priorities (reach for these first)

### 1. Idiolect — every named voice is identifiable
Each recurring character has a recognizable way of talking. Before writing their
lines, know their **voice card** (below). The fastest way to kill "boring" is to
stop letting characters share a default voice.

### 2. POV-colored narration — the prose takes the character's side
The trilogy is **close-third and multi-POV** (book one rides Kester; book two
splits between Pira on Korya and Sable's group; book three widens). In any scene,
the *narration itself* borrows the POV character's diction, biases, and blind
spots — not just their dialogue. The same room, the same person, should read
differently depending on whose head we are in.
- Kester sees a corridor and counts what it costs to run; Sable sees cover and
  exits; Hessa sees who in it is dying.
- A POV character mis-reads others on the page — that gap is characterization.
- Never let two POV chapters sound like the same narrator.

### 3. Wit and vulnerability — the armor and the crack
The most alive moments are a character's **defense** and the **failure** of it.
- **Wit as armor:** humor that is a *defense and a display of intelligence*, and
  that exposes the wound *through* the cleverness rather than hiding it. Dry,
  dark, sparing — never modern quip-banter.
- **The crack:** a composed character whose guard slips for one line, then
  re-seals. Ration these. One real crack outweighs a page of feeling.

---

## The idiolect toolbox

The levers that make a voice. Pull different ones for different people.

- **Diction** — vocabulary set by education, culture, trade, and *who they think
  they're talking to*. The same character speaks differently to a superior, a
  child, and an enemy.
- **Rhythm** — clipped, rolling, halting, smooth. The music of the voice.
- **Sentence length & shape** — terse vs. periodic; do they finish sentences,
  interrupt, trail off?
- **Verbal tics & signatures** — a recurring construction, a word they overuse, a
  thing they always do (correct you, name specifics, ask before answering).
- **What they refuse to say** — every voice has a no-go: a subject they deflect, a
  word they won't use, a feeling they won't name. The evasion *is* the character.
- **The goal under the line** — every line is a move toward something the
  character wants. Dialogue is never "self-expression"; it is action.
- **Consistency** — a voice holds unless there is a reason to break it; the break
  is then meaningful.

## House registers — make cultures sound as distinct as they're named

The trilogy's naming registers extend into *speech*. A Vendine and a Keris should
sound as different as they look on the page.

| Group | Register of speech |
|---|---|
| **The Vendine** (Orrel, Aemilius, Caradec) | Formal, ceremonial, control. The diction of "the form" — duty, completion, honor. Understatement; emotion is improper. |
| **Latinate aristocracy / Old Houses** (Aurelius, the Synedrion's senior figures) | Measured, Roman gravitas; long view; allusive. |
| **Persian-register outsiders** (Xerxes, Cyrus) | Risen-from-outside precision; intelligence as currency; no inherited ease. |
| **The Keris** (Imir, Kazimir, Ivana) | Plainer, communal, martial. "We" before "I." Direct, unsentimental, patient or impatient by generation. |
| **The Sisters** (Hessa) | Quiet, slow, attentive to thresholds. Fewer words than expected. Never preaches. |
| **Frontier / lower terraces** (Roan, frontier figures) | Terse, concrete, transactional. Courtesy that can also be threat. |

Register is carried by **word choice and syntax, never by phonetic spelling.** We
do not write "Yeh know." A Keris fighter and a Vendine lord differ in *what* and
*how* they say, not in misspelled sounds.

## Mechanics (the house rules)

- **Quotes & tags.** Standard double quotes. Minimize "said"; prefer an **action
  beat** that doubles as a *tell* (*his left hand was resting on his cuff*).
- **No adverb-tags.** Don't prop a line up with "he said angrily." If the line and
  the beat already carry the feeling, the tag is dead weight. Reach for a tag only
  when the line alone is genuinely ambiguous.
- **Italics** for spoken emphasis (*correctional facility*) and for internal or
  remembered lines (*Do you sleep, Mr. Vaile?*). Don't overuse.
- **Em-dash** for interruption; **ellipsis** for trailing off. Different acts —
  one is taken from them, one is given up.
- **No phonetic dialect spelling.** Register via diction and syntax (see above).
- **The declarative-question device.** Controlled, cold characters often state a
  question without a question mark — "And the catch." / "So what's the plan." It
  signals a person who does not ask so much as *require*. This is a **per-character
  tool, not a house default** — using it everywhere flattens voices. Who uses it:
  Xerxes, Quintus, Sable, Kester, Roan. Who doesn't: Cassian (real questions,
  banter), Hessa (gentle, genuine questions), Bastien (theatrical).
- **Profanity** is sparing and register-appropriate: a frontier enforcer may; a
  Sister will not; the Vendine consider crudeness a lapse of form.
- **Subtext vs. directness.** Default to subtext — people say less than they mean.
  *Ration* the naked, direct line so that when it finally comes, it lands like a
  blow. (Sable: "My brother was in Wing Seven.")
- **No melodrama.** Hold complications open; do not let characters announce the
  theme or weep on cue. The cooler the surface, the harder the depth hits.

## Voice card — template

For each major character, fill:

- **Register / origin** — culture and class the voice comes from.
- **Diction** — vocabulary, what they reach for.
- **Rhythm & sentence shape** — the music and length.
- **Tics / signatures** — the recurring move.
- **What they refuse to say** — the evasion.
- **Humor** — kind and amount.
- **Composed vs. under pressure** — how the voice changes when cornered.
- **How others hear them vs. their self-image** — the gap.
- **Signature line** — one line only they would say.

## Voice cards

### Xerxes — the Architect (Summum)
- **Register/origin:** Persian-register outsider risen into an Old House; lifetime in narrative control.
- **Diction:** precise, abstract, institutional; speaks of order, necessity, the long view.
- **Rhythm:** even, unhurried; never raises the voice; pauses are chosen, not lost.
- **Tics:** asks questions he already knows the answer to, to watch you answer; flatters exactly.
- **Refuses:** his wife and child, the curse he believes he carries; any admission of error.
- **Humor:** cold surgical irony, aimed to test.
- **Under pressure:** *more* controlled, not less — the calm tightens.
- **Others vs. self:** others feel handled and don't know how; he believes he is the only adult in the room.
- **Signature line:** *"Tell me what you think you know. I'll tell you which parts are true."*

### Marella Vren — Dux Vren, the Narrine
- **Register/origin:** Communications arm; a press editor who became its director.
- **Diction:** editorial, economical; she chooses the load-bearing word and discards the rest.
- **Rhythm:** clipped, final; sentences close like a door.
- **Tics:** corrects your phrasing; reframes what you said into what she'll allow it to mean.
- **Refuses:** sentiment; the foundational crime she covered for Xerxes.
- **Humor:** dry, editorial, never warm by accident.
- **Under pressure:** colder and shorter; warmth withdrawn entirely.
- **Others vs. self:** others find her chilling; she believes her management is mercy — *the knife and the bandage*.
- **Signature line:** *"That's not the word. The word is 'containment.' Use it."*

### Bastien Orrel — the Warden
- **Register/origin:** Vendine, House Orrel; ceremonial diction warped by a wound.
- **Diction:** courtly, religious, warm on the surface; the language of family and forgiveness used as a blade.
- **Rhythm:** expansive, performative; he enjoys his own lines.
- **Tics:** calls people *family* and by first name; the warm smile that doesn't move.
- **Refuses:** that the Cleanse is wrong (his cathedral); his father's contempt; his grandfather's letter being anything but endorsement.
- **Humor:** delighted, theatrical cruelty — exhilaration at the worst moment.
- **Under pressure:** *more* theatrical, almost joyful (the trench taught him to laugh at the edge).
- **Others vs. self:** others meet menace dressed as love; he believes he is a saint who corrected an error.
- **Signature line:** *"I loved you like a son. Are you listening?"*

### Quintus Aemilius — Vendine Council Chair
- **Register/origin:** Vendine, oldest formality; fifty years in council rooms.
- **Diction:** spare, exact; the fewest words that will hold.
- **Rhythm:** long silences, then one brief intervention that turns the room.
- **Tics:** lets a question hang for hours so the answer carries the weight of having been waited for; speaks last.
- **Refuses:** to commit early; to show feeling; the lie he tells himself about the form.
- **Humor:** almost none; the driest possible understatement.
- **Under pressure:** *more* silent — silence is his instrument.
- **Others vs. self:** juniors mistake his quiet for assent; he is manipulating the room through patience.
- **Signature line:** *(after a long silence)* *"No."*

### Sable Reyek — leader of the Undersiders
- **Register/origin:** prison/working underclass; grief made into command.
- **Diction:** blunt, concrete, no softening; moral words used plainly.
- **Rhythm:** declarative, flat, fast when it counts.
- **Tics:** states the hard truth, then acts; will not let a comfortable lie stand.
- **Refuses:** cheap forgiveness; false hope; performing her own pain.
- **Humor:** gallows — grim, exact, never light.
- **Under pressure:** *calmer* and more decisive; the math is already done.
- **Others vs. self:** others read her as cold; she is the only one still counting the dead by name.
- **Signature line:** *"I don't forgive you. I want that clear."*

### Kester Vaile — the conscience
- **Register/origin:** Hegemony administrator; bureaucratic precision turned to penance.
- **Diction:** factual, exact; he reaches for the record — names, serials, days.
- **Rhythm:** flat, economical, withholding; feeling kept just offstage.
- **Tics:** recites specifics as a moral act ("Prisoner 8841-C. He made it forty-one days.").
- **Refuses:** to perform emotion; to forgive himself; the secret he carries (the shuttle, Pira).
- **Humor:** bleak, self-lacerating, rare.
- **Under pressure:** *more* precise — he retreats into facts.
- **Others vs. self:** others see a complicit paper-pusher; he sees a man who read every file and did nothing for nine years.
- **Signature line:** *"I read every one. I told myself if I remembered them, it meant something."*

### Mother Hessa — Sisters of the Long Patience
- **Register/origin:** old Andra Prime middle terraces; fifty years of *being with*.
- **Diction:** plain, small, specific; the vocabulary of thresholds, not doctrine.
- **Rhythm:** quiet and slow; fewer words than people expect; she lets silence do work.
- **Tics:** answers a large question with a small, exact one about a single life; never endorses, never preaches.
- **Refuses:** to judge aloud; to say what she has long suspected (until forced to).
- **Humor:** gentle, wry, rare.
- **Under pressure:** *quieter* — people have to lean in to hear her.
- **Others vs. self:** some read her warmth as performance; she knows her patience has not been innocent.
- **Signature line:** *"Diuturnum patientia."* *(or:)* *"And the child — does she sleep?"*

### Cassian Vren — editor of *The Andra Meridian*
- **Register/origin:** Communications arm; urbane, privileged, dimmed.
- **Diction:** clever, allusive, deflective; reaches for the joke that is also true.
- **Rhythm:** easy, quick, glancing; he keeps it light to keep it shallow.
- **Tics:** answers a serious question with wit; sees the manipulation and names it out loud.
- **Refuses:** earnestness; his grief for the father who died when he was small.
- **Humor:** the trilogy's wittiest voice — urbane, self-aware, a Tyrion register.
- **Under pressure:** *more* flippant — until the one moment the wit fails and something true falls out.
- **Others vs. self:** others read a wastrel; he is harder to move than anyone believes, and chose this life on purpose.
- **Signature line:** *"You want me to risk my neck for the truth. I don't even risk it for breakfast."*

### Kazimir — the Keris son
- **Register/origin:** Keris (Slavic-register, communal, martial); raised to fight, raised in exile.
- **Diction:** direct, hard, strategic; "we" and "the bloodline" before "I."
- **Rhythm:** military economy; impatience held on a short leash.
- **Tics:** turns a moral question into a tactical one; measures people as assets.
- **Refuses:** sentiment about the enemy's dead; his father's patience.
- **Humor:** little; hard irony when it comes.
- **Under pressure:** pushes *toward* action; the leash strains.
- **Others vs. self:** others see a hard commander; he sees a father refusing to hand his children the exile he inherited.
- **Signature line:** *"My father waited his whole life. I will not give my daughter the same inheritance."*

### Roan — the eastern-quarter boss
- **Register/origin:** Andra Prime lower terraces; came up parentless, built it himself.
- **Diction:** terse, concrete, transactional; plain words, no flourish.
- **Rhythm:** short, even; courtesy with a floor of threat under it.
- **Tics:** states the price; draws the one line he will not cross (children).
- **Refuses:** to speak of his murdered wife — *the name they don't speak of*; sentiment.
- **Humor:** dry, street, economical.
- **Under pressure:** *quieter* — the calm gets dangerous.
- **Others vs. self:** others see a criminal; he sees a man keeping the only order the lower ground has.
- **Signature line:** *"Anything but the children. You bring me children, we're done — and you don't want us done."*

## Voice cards — the wider cast

*Condensed cards (Voice / Refuses / Under pressure & humor / Signature). The full
template above is reserved for the ten principal voices; expand any of these as the
character earns more dialogue. Grounded in the Part II character entries.*

### House Orrel & House Aemilius (Vendine)

**Aldric Orrel — the dying patriarch.**
- *Voice:* cold Vendine formality, sparse; disappointment delivered by omission, not attack; long silences that function as judgments.
- *Refuses:* to praise Bastien; to speak of Mireille (46 years of buried grief); to name the favoritism.
- *Pressure/humor:* colder and shorter; no humor.
- *Signature:* *"Your brother understands what the house requires."*

**Roderic Orrel — the favored heir.**
- *Voice:* correct Vendine, in his father's image — but warmer the moment Aldric leaves the room; a private decency that leaks out as small honesty.
- *Refuses:* to openly question the favoritism he profited from; to disown Bastien.
- *Pressure/humor:* retreats into correctness; light, careful humor.
- *Signature:* *"I won't pretend I earned it. But I won't burn it either."*

**Aurelan Orrel — the internal dissenter.**
- *Voice:* cadet-branch, scholarly; precise, interpretive; reads people and documents the same careful way.
- *Refuses:* to say her reading aloud (until late); to comfort with the family myth.
- *Pressure/humor:* steadier as it sharpens; dry.
- *Signature:* *"It was an apology, Bastien. Not a blessing. You built a cathedral on a man's grief."*

**Marcus Aemilius — the great warrior.**
- *Voice:* plain, physical, concrete; a man who says little because the body says it; won't chase a subject.
- *Refuses:* to discuss Bastien; to take a side; sentiment.
- *Pressure/humor:* withdraws into silence/the colosseum; dry soldier's humor.
- *Signature:* *"I fight. I don't make speeches about it."*

**Lavinia Aemilius — the matriarch.**
- *Voice:* high-social Vendine; voluble, warm, surface-bright; fills silences; assumes the best.
- *Refuses:* to look hard at her sister; to see the dark under the family.
- *Pressure/humor:* chatters more; easy social humor.
- *Signature:* *"Octavia? Oh, she's the quiet one. She handles things."*

**Octavia Aemilius — the hidden hand.**
- *Voice:* institutionally invisible; minimal, exact; answers narrowly, volunteers nothing; an operator's calm.
- *Refuses:* anything about her work; any warmth that could be traced.
- *Pressure/humor:* unchanged — that is the warning; none.
- *Signature:* *"I'll see to it."*

**Tancred Caradec — the dissenting house head.**
- *Voice:* Vendine, formal and hard; speaks of the *form* as a living thing; invokes his dead brother obliquely.
- *Refuses:* to say it publicly (until book three); to debase the form.
- *Pressure/humor:* austere, controlled; grim.
- *Signature:* *"A warrior chooses the form. Those children chose nothing. That is not our way — it is the end of it."*

### The Asclepi & the Limine (medical, frontier)

**Aelia Mardin — Medical Director.**
- *Voice:* cosmopolitan, clinical, diplomatic; the connector who has spoken to everyone; turns hard questions into procedure; looks away *in language*.
- *Refuses:* her infertility and her husband's affairs; what the augmentation costs the children.
- *Pressure/humor:* smoother and more clinical (composure is the tell); cool dry wit.
- *Signature:* *"Let's not be dramatic. It's a procedure, and procedures have outcomes."*

**Professor Cyrus — senior researcher.**
- *Voice:* pre-Hegemony precision (a register-cousin to Xerxes); exact, qualified, weary; self-justifies — *without me it would be worse.*
- *Refuses:* to name the dead children plainly; to admit the threat to his daughters governs him.
- *Pressure/humor:* retreats into data and caveats; bleak academic dryness.
- *Signature:* *"I have kept the records accurate. Someone should. Someone who minds."*

**Edric — Frontier senior coordinator.**
- *Voice:* managerial, evasive, built to deny; constructs plausible ignorance in real time; clipped and cold to his son, a hidden tenderness only for the married woman.
- *Refuses:* to know more than he must; to show the one love he has.
- *Pressure/humor:* more procedural — *that was below my level*; thin defensive humor.
- *Signature:* *"I oversee the territories. I don't read every requisition. That's rather the point."*

**Raknir Vehl — regional governor.**
- *Voice:* frontier authority, controlled, weighty; the diplomat's polish from old trade work; the man who brings order.
- *Refuses:* weakness; to fall fully under the syndicate.
- *Pressure/humor:* lordly calm; dry.
- *Signature:* *"This world ran on blood before I came. Now it runs on my word. Choose which you'd prefer."*

**Atticus Vehl — the disavowed son.**
- *Voice:* frontier-rough, cynical, honorless; blunt, mocking, transactional; needles authority.
- *Refuses:* respect; his father's name with any warmth.
- *Pressure/humor:* sharper, crueler; quick cruel humor.
- *Signature:* *"My father keeps order. I keep the things order can't be seen touching."*

### The Synedrion

**Aurelius — Old Houses (House Corvinus).**
- *Voice:* Latinate, scholarly, archival; allusive and periodic; cites papers and precedent; the cousin who could have been Summum, grief under the dryness.
- *Refuses:* to claim the place he lost; to act until forced.
- *Pressure/humor:* steadier, sadder; dry bookish wit.
- *Signature:* *"My uncle chose Xerxes. I have spent thirty years deciding whether he was wrong. I have my answer now."*

**Mistress Veliya — the Healer.**
- *Voice:* the old healing tradition; plain, bodily, unhurried; older than Hessa and blunter about death; says the plain thing softly.
- *Refuses:* the Medical arm's euphemisms; to pretend the split was ever legitimate.
- *Pressure/humor:* unhurried; dry, earthy.
- *Signature:* *"They call it a procedure. I have washed what their procedures leave. It has another name."*

### The Keris of Korya

**Imir — the grandfather.**
- *Voice:* eldest Keris; slow, parable-shaped, the long view; answers with a lesson or a question; lets time argue for him.
- *Refuses:* haste; to hate cleanly; to strike before the blow can be fatal.
- *Pressure/humor:* *slower*, not faster; gentle ancient humor.
- *Signature:* *"My grandfather was a slave. I am not. We did this once, slowly. We will do it once more."*

**Kazimir's daughter — Ivana (~7).**
- *Voice:* Keris child, fighter-trained; proud, blunt, literal; treats fighting as ordinary; corrects the outsider.
- *Refuses:* softness; being told she's too small.
- *Pressure/humor:* fiercer; hard kid's bravado.
- *Signature:* *"Hold it like this. Not like that. You'll lose the hand you hold it wrong with."*

**Nadya — Pira's best friend (9).**
- *Voice:* ordinary Keris child; warm, chattering, kind; the caves are simply home; explains the world to Pira without weight.
- *Refuses:* very little — she's the one place with no wall.
- *Pressure/humor:* bright child's humor.
- *Signature:* *"Everyone's scared of the dark down here at first. Then it's just home. You'll see."*

**Pira — the ward (6 in book two).**
- *Voice:* a child between two worlds; Keris dialect now, Compact memories fading; spare, watchful; grief surfaces sideways (the stuffed animal, the names she keeps).
- *Refuses:* to talk about the crash directly; to call anyone *mother*.
- *Pressure/humor:* goes quiet and still; small, rare, surprising humor.
- *Signature:* *"Sable said she'd remember. Every day. So I do too."*

### The resistance

**Resh — Sable's protectee (18).**
- *Voice:* near-silent; when he speaks, spare and raw; one short true sentence after long silence; speaks to Mouse when he won't speak to others.
- *Refuses:* almost all speech; his grief, except sideways.
- *Pressure/humor:* shuts down further; a rare dark flicker.
- *Signature:* *(after a long silence)* *"I knew her. Before. I think I knew her."*

**Mouse — the prototype (horror-object).**
- *Voice:* programmed flatness; compliance-speech, literal, present-tense; repeats instructions; a near-human crack only when Resh is close.
- *Refuses:* (cannot) — the programming refuses *for* her; the horror is what has been removed.
- *Pressure/humor:* flatter, more compliant — until the single late POV beat where the conditioning wavers; no humor, and the absence is the point.
- *Signature:* *"I am ready for the next instruction."* — and, once, off-script: *"…Resh."*

## Before / after — the method in action

**1. Quintus (patience, the declarative-question device, silence as instrument)**
- *Flat:* "I don't think we should approve this. It's too risky. What do you all think?"
- *Round:* He let the question sit until the table grew uncomfortable. Then, once: "Whose son leads the second cohort." It was not a question. The officer who had brought the proposal began, very quietly, to withdraw it.

**2. Hessa (the small exact question; subtext; quiet)**
- *Flat:* "I'm worried about what's happening to the children in that program. It's terrible."
- *Round:* "The new recruits," she said. People leaned in. "They don't flinch at the bell. The old ones always flinched." She folded her hands. "Has anyone asked where the flinch went."

**3. Cassian (wit as armor; the crack)**
- *Flat:* "I'm scared to leak this. My mother controls everything and I don't want to lose my position."
- *Round:* "You want me to set fire to my own house," he said, refilling his glass, "and you've brought no wine for the occasion." He drank. Then, not looking at her: "She'd know it was me by the second sentence. She taught me the sentences." The glass stopped halfway down.

**4. Roan (transactional courtesy; the line he won't cross)**
- *Flat:* "I can help you find your daughter, but I'll need something in return. Also, I don't deal with anything involving kids."
- *Round:* "A father looking for a daughter." Roan turned the cup, didn't drink. "That, I can move on. It'll cost you, and you'll pay it." He set the cup down. "One thing first, so we understand each other. She's grown?" Kester said yes. "Good," Roan said. "I don't do children."
