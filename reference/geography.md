# The Tartaros Cycle — Geography

*The canonical map of the trilogy: every world that matters, where it sits, its
climate and weather, what it is for, and who lives there. This file exists because
the worlds were scattered across the bible and easy to misread — so the map is now
**set in stone here.** Full character detail still lives in
`../bible/TARTAROS_CYCLE_BIBLE.md` (Part I §8, Part III); this file is the
consolidated, authoritative geography.*

**Arrangement (locked 2026-06-18):** the trilogy keeps a **five-world core** and
treats everything else as minor/peripheral, named only as a scene needs it. The
core is **Andra Prime, Tartaros, Verdaine, Elysse, Korya.** This is deliberately
lean — the politics (distributed arms, a capital everyone must travel to) is the
load-bearing fact, not a long planet-list.

**The thing that used to cause confusion — read first:** there is **one** prison
world, **Tartaros**, and the hidden Cleanse facility — **the Harvester** — sits on
it. The public **prison** (Facility Nine and its complex) and the **Harvester**
(the secret production facility, a terraformed enclave) are **two locations on the
same world**, far apart. The **Undersiders** live beneath the prison complex.
**Book one ends with the five protagonists held at the Harvester enclave on
Tartaros.**

## 2. The four arms are not on one world

The Homonoia's power is **distributed across worlds** — which is *why* it works the
way it does. No single planet holds it; representatives must **travel** to Andra
Prime for the Synedrion, and the Narrine sits at the capital precisely to hold one
narrative across many worlds.

| Arm | Seat / base | Notes |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| **The Vendine** (defense) | **Verdaine** (homeworld) | Hereditary houses; ceremonial aristocracy. The Sisters of the Long Patience are embedded here. |
| **The Asclepi** (medical) | **Elysse** (homeworld) | Also **runs the Harvester** — the hidden Cleanse enclave on Tartaros. Its seat and its secret works are on different worlds. |
| **The Limine** (frontier) | **No single homeworld** — dispersed territories | Operates **Tartaros** (the prison) and the Cleanse's seven sites; chief named territory is **Vallum** (Raknir Vehl). Power is regional, money mixed. Faces the Federation across the frontier. |
| **The Narrine** (communications) | **Andra Prime** (the capital) | Most politically embedded; controls what daily citizens know. Maintains the **Federation-threat narrative** (§6) and contained the book-one broadcast. |

**Cross-cutting institutions, also on Andra Prime:**
- **The Architect / Homonoia coordination** — Xerxes is politically embedded across
  all four arms; his working seat is the capital. *[Author-decided, pending review.]*
- **The Synedrion** — meets in **the Tholos**, a circular pre-Homonoia hall on Andra
  Prime (nine chairs; the Keris chair empty). **[CANON]**
- **The Sisters of the Long Patience** — embedded with the Vendine on Verdaine; also
  run **Mother Hessa's clinic on Andra Prime** (lower terraces). **[CANON]**
- **The Mercantile Guilds** — the economic estate; administer Hegemony currency and
  trade (§7); hold the worn Synedrion chair.

## 3. The carceral system — set in stone

The piece most prone to being misread. **One world, three layers, plus the anchor.**

### Tartaros — the prison world
- **[CANON]** A cold, near-airless **prison moon orbiting Moloch**. The public
  destination for the convicted: *commit crimes, you are shipped off-world to
  Tartaros.* Run by the **Limine**; **Bastien Orrel is its Warden**.
- **Prison locations:** Facility Nine (the prison), Wing Seven (the medical wing of
  the abuses), Building Twelve (the children's facility), the underground palace (the
  Asclepi's hidden chamber complex, Elysse-style architecture), the records archive
  (Chayne's duct, where Pira survived). Pier Three is a surface launch facility ~40km
  north of Facility Nine, over the Vorrath Range.

### The Undersiders — beneath the prison
- **[Author-decided, pending review — locked]** The **underclass who live in the
  under-levels beneath the Tartaros prison complex** — a service-and-labor
  population, the families and forgotten of the carceral world. **Sable Reyek's
  resistance is born here** (her brother Tomas died at Wing Seven). Hidden, mutually
  protective, invisible to Andra Prime's daily citizens — which is why a vanished
  Undersider becomes a *resistance martyr-story*, not a public scandal.

### The Harvester *(public name: the Conservatory)* — the hidden enclave
- **[CANON, revised]** The Cleanse's hidden **production facility**: a **terraformed,
  walled green enclave on Tartaros itself**, far from the prison (a ~14-hour transit
  "around the curve of the world"; its coordinates struck from the charts). Warm,
  lush, engineered against the dead cold around it — an artificial Conservatory on a
  dead world. Run by the **Asclepi**. *Not a separate planet.*
- **Harvester locations:** the receiving bay/pad, the residence wing (**where book one
  ends**), the dining room (the ch10–11 dinner), Bastien's quarters (the grandfather's
  letter), the gardens (terraformed sixty years ago, restarted by the Board fifteen
  years ago).
- **This is where the five are held at the end of book one** — Sable, Kester, Resh,
  Davour in the residence wing; **Mouse held separately by the Architect's people.**

### Moloch — the anchor
- **[Author-decided, pending review]** A cold gas giant; uninhabitable; Tartaros is
  its prison moon. Defines the remote penal system rather than being settled itself.

### How it connects (and the book-two implication)
- The move from the prison/Undersider levels to the Harvester is now an **intra-world
  transit** (Pier Three → ~14h across Tartaros → the enclave), not interplanetary.
- The interplanetary route that matters is **Tartaros ↔ Andra Prime** (the ch9 shuttle
  left Pier Three for Andra Prime and was ordered shot down "as it reached Andra
  Prime"; the Keris pulled it to Korya). **[CANON]**
- **Getting off-world is interplanetary, not a corridor.** Leaving Tartaros means a
  ship, and ships are controlled by the authorities — the realistic constraint that
  shapes book two's opening (deliberate ship-out vs. earned off-world escape).

## 4. World profiles (core)

### Andra Prime — the home world
- **Role:** Hegemony capital; the "earth-equivalent" world of daily life. Seat of the
  **Narrine**, the **Synedrion** (the Tholos), the **Architect**, and the **Mercantile
  Guilds**.
- **Climate & weather:** Temperate; genuine seasons; a dense urban microclimate —
  cleaner air and light on the upper terraces, industrial haze and "calm"-numbed
  flatness in the lower ones.
- **Terrain:** A **layered terrace city** — social rank is literally vertical (§5).
- **Residents:** The daily citizenry ("lowside" in the author's sense — ordinary
  people, *not* the Undersiders); Marella, Cassian & Severine Vren (Narrine);
  Aurelius (Old Houses residence); Lyra Orrel and Lira Vaile (both third terrace);
  Mother Hessa (clinic, lower terraces); **Roan** (eastern-quarter syndicate).
- **Key locations:** Cresswell University; the third terrace; the eastern quarter &
  the coffee house; the Tholos; the Information Bureau [TBD]; the Reform Commission [TBD].
- **Story role:** Book two's daily-citizen battlefield — the Narrine's containment, the
  Kester/Roan/Lira thread, the search for Pira.

### Tartaros — see §3. *(Prison world; Undersiders beneath; the Harvester enclave; Bastien the Warden.)*

### Verdaine — the Vendine homeworld
- **Climate & weather:** Warm and wet; high humidity, frequent rain, slow rivers that
  flood; long green growing seasons. Lush jungle-and-wetland with great trees.
- **Residents:** The Vendine houses — Orrel, Aemilius, Caradec, and the other senior
  houses; ceremonial aristocracy with spartan internal discipline; the Sisters embedded.
- **Story role:** The defense arm's culture and the duels (the augmented-men scandal
  Hessa has tracked for forty years); ancestral seat of Bastien's house.

### Elysse — the Asclepi homeworld
- **Climate & weather:** Engineered tropical — managed, *too curated to be natural*;
  no real weather, only maintained perfection.
- **Residents:** Asclepi leadership (Aelia Mardin), senior researchers.
- **Story role:** The medical arm's polished face; the architectural source of both the
  underground palace and the Harvester enclave's engineered green.

### Korya — the Keris homeworld (hidden refuge)
- **Climate & weather:** Cold, *biological* cold. Subarctic tundra above — snow, wind,
  long deep winters; below, geothermally warmed, stable cave-cities.
- **Terrain:** Deceptive — small, primitive-looking mountain settlements above hide an
  industrial, politically coordinated cave-civilization below.
- **Residents:** The Keris bloodline (Imir, Kazimir, Milana, Ivana) and people (Nadya's
  family, ordinary cave-born Keris); **Asham and Pira**.
- **Key locations:** The mountain settlements (what surveys see and leave); the
  cave-cities; Pira's home with Asham [TBD: which cave-city]; the grandfather's seat
  [TBD: which cave-city].
- **Story role:** Thread A's home; the convergence that closes *The Ward of Korya*; the
  unsurveyed world the protagonists reach without knowing Pira is there. **Not the
  Keris's ancestral seat — their refuge** (see §8).

### Minor worlds — Vallum, Sephara
- **Vallum** *[Author-decided, pending review]* — arid, dust-storm-prone, mineral-rich;
  a **walled, rich city-world** with a mining moon. The Vehl family power base;
  prisoner laborers (the profitable share); Atticus Vehl's contraband sub-syndicate.
  The Limine's most prominent named territory; the hereditary **city-key** *is* the
  right to govern.
- **Sephara** — temperate, unremarkable, provincial. **Chayne** kept its municipal
  archives thirty-one years; Tomas Reyek's daughter **Mira**; the guard **Renko's**
  family live here. The plain bureaucratic counterpoint to the arm-seat capitals.

## 5. Andra Prime's terraces — the social ladder

Rank on the capital is **literally vertical**. Three tiers, and they matter for who
notices a disappearance (the realism of book two's opening):

- **Upper terraces / the polished political center** — the institutional-aristocratic
  top: the arms' offices, the Synedrion, the grand residences. Notices through
  *official* channels.
- **Middle terraces** — the respectable middle class. **Lira** (third terrace) and
  **Lyra Orrel** live here; **Hessa** came from a middle district since *hollowed out*
  by the conglomerate. This tier **looks away**; it neither riots nor martyrs.
- **Lower / "bad" terraces** — the underclass of the capital: **Roan's** eastern
  quarter, "calm"-numbed workers, parentless kids, Hessa's clinic. Frontier-terse;
  transactional; protective of neighborhood.

**Note on "lowside":** in the author's usage, **lowside = ordinary daily citizens**
(the general surface populace the Narrine manages) — a *different* axis from the
**Undersiders** (the hidden under-prison underclass of Tartaros). Andra Prime's
terraces are the lowside's vertical ladder; the Undersiders are not on it at all.

## 6. The Federation — the external rival (and the manufactured enemy)

**[Author-decided, pending review — political shape now closed]**

The Federation is the Hegemony's external rival **and the public face of the war the
Cleanse is supposedly built to fight.** The citizens of Andra Prime cannot be told
the true target (the officially-extinct Keris); naming the Keris would resurrect the
buried history. So the **Narrine** points the public at the Federation instead — a
real but diminished polity inflated into an existential threat.

> **The three-shell enemy.** Public (citizens): *the Federation.* Inner Homonoia
> (Xerxes & the elite): *the returning Keris.* The truth: *Xerxes manufactured the
> Keris's return himself.* The Federation is the outermost mask. See the load-bearing
> ironies in `TRILOGY_SYNOPSIS.md`.

### Where they reside
**Beyond the Hegemony's frontier — out past the Limine's border territories.**

> **Core** (Andra Prime · Verdaine · Elysse) → **Limine frontier** (Tartaros, Vallum,
> the dispersed sites) → **the edge** → **the Federation** (Cairn · Halden · Auster ·
> scattered affiliates)

You reach it by going *out*, through the frontier. This is why the Narrine can
credibly claim they are "massing at the borders," why the resistance's
refuge/archive-to-the-Federation logic holds, and why the **Limine** (the arm that
*faces* the false enemy) is also the arm that runs the prisons — its reach justified
by the very threat the Homonoia manufactured.

### Political shape (closed — valid, kept light)
- **Type:** a **loose confederation by voluntary compact** — the structural opposite
  of the Homonoia. Member worlds keep full sovereignty; they cooperate, they don't obey.
- **Governance:** a standing **assembly on Cairn** (the hub); delegates from member
  worlds and affiliates; **decisions by consensus**, so it is slow and rarely speaks
  with one voice. No single ruler; leadership is representative and rotating.
- **Military:** **no central fleet** — member and affiliate militias; joint action is
  ad hoc. *(This is why Mireille Orrel died in a "Federation-**affiliated** raid" —
  affiliate-driven, not a unified war fleet.)*
- **Economy:** an **independent frontier trade network** (see §7) — commerce, not a
  command economy; quiet, unsanctioned trade across the Hegemony frontier.
- **Founding ethos:** the worlds that **refused or slipped the consolidation** two
  centuries ago. Anti-centralization is their identity — hence the plain naming
  (Cairn, Halden, Auster), the deliberate opposite of Latinate-Greek grandeur.
- **Posture to the Hegemony:** a **cold frontier** — intermittent friction, harbors
  defectors and refugees, but **materially incapable of threatening the Hegemony core.**
  *(That incapacity is the engine of the lie: the Narrine must inflate them precisely
  because they aren't actually a danger.)*
- **Knowledge:** **outside the conspiracy** — the Federation does not know the Cleanse's
  true nature and has **no connection to the Keris.** A real polity, not a secret player.
- **A seam inside the border:** some frontier populations within Limine territory quietly
  lean Federation — the propaganda's "enemy within," and the resistance's thin thread of
  real contacts.

## 7. Trade & currency

**[Author-decided, pending review]** Money is another of the Homonoia's grips on daily
life. Defined to three tiers — no deeper.

1. **A single centralized currency — the *denar*** — administered by the **Mercantile
   Guilds** under Homonoia oversight. A consolidated empire consolidates its money; the
   Guilds are the economic estate that runs it (and finally have a function behind their
   Synedrion chair).
2. **Frontier company-scrip.** The Limine worlds are "company-town-meets-prison-colony,
   life dependent on conglomerate infrastructure." Frontier and prison labor (Vallum's
   prisoner-miners, the "calm"-numbed workers) is paid and bound in **company credit**,
   not hard currency. Money as a **leash**, not just a medium.
3. **The shadow economy** — syndicates, barter, favors. Roan's syndicate moves money
   outside the official channel and already funds Hessa for standing. **This is where the
   resistance operates** (the plumbing of book two's survival).

**Across the border:** the Federation runs its **own independent trade network**, outside
Hegemony money — so crossing the frontier is crossing an economic boundary too, which is
why frontier trade is quiet and unsanctioned.

**Deliberately not defined:** exchange rates, prices, denominations, Federation currency
names. Textural only.

## 8. The Keris — before the fall, and now

**[Author-decided, pending review — Reading 1]**

- **Before the fall:** the Keris were a house at the **institutional center of the
  Hegemony**, governing from **Andra Prime** — the only house to hold *both* an executive
  arm (**the Adjudicate**) and a **Synedrion seat** simultaneously. Their power was their
  *seats*, not a separate homeworld.
- **The fall (~HCE 2178):** Xerxes's manufactured evidence enabled the consolidation; the
  Adjudicate was disbanded, the Synedrion seat left empty, the house nearly destroyed.
- **Now:** survivors **fled to Korya** — a remote, cold world the frontier surveys ignore.
  **Korya is their refuge that became home, not their ancestral seat.**
- **Why it matters:** their restoration in the final book (the Adjudicate reborn, the empty
  chair filled) is a **homecoming to the center on Andra Prime** — reclaiming the seat that
  was always theirs — not the founding of a new state. The empty chair held their place at
  the center the whole time.
