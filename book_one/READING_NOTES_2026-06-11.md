## Tier 1 — Hard canon contradictions & author decisions

### 1. Lira's homeworld is inconsistent — and Sol-system names leak in **[DECISION/HARD]**
- ch2 (`...ch2.md:6–9`): Lira on **Europa**, not seen in **six years**, "turned **twelve**" — frames her as a child.
- ch4 (`...ch4.md:258`), ch5 (`...ch5.md:266–270`): **Europa**, "under-ice canals," water-skids.
- ch3 (`...ch3.md:68`), ch8 (`...ch8.md:204,310`): correctly **Andra Prime**, age **22**.
- Bible: Lira is **22, Andra Prime (third terrace), Cresswell University, not seen 11 years, stopped writing HCE 2371.** Book two's whole Lira storyline (eastern-quarter coffee house, the Roan trail) runs through Andra Prime.
- Related: **Europa, Ganymede, Phobos, "Martian" brandy** (ch1, ch2:40, ch4, ch5, ch11:42) are real Sol-system names that clash with the invented cosmology (Andra Prime, Verdaine, Elysse, Korya, Cairn).
- **Recommendation:** relocate Lira to Andra Prime, fix duration to 11 years and age to adult, and replace all Sol-system place names with invented worlds. This is nearly forced by book two.

### 2. Chayne's origin world is **Korya** **[DECISION/HARD]**
- ch4 (`...ch4.md:66`): "I was a records officer… on **Korya**. I worked in municipal archives for thirty-one years." Reinforced ch8 (`...ch8.md:180`): a "former **Korya** municipal-archives colleague."
- Bible: **Korya is the Keris homeworld** — hidden, surveyed-and-left-alone, no Hegemony municipal apparatus. An ordinary civic records clerk openly "from Korya" can't coexist with that.
- **Options:** (a) rename Chayne's origin to a settled Hegemony world; (b) lean in — make Chayne quietly Keris-connected, which would *seed* the archive's eventual journey to Korya (richer, but complicates the Keris's hidden status).

### 3. "Sennae Vorlith" — first name not in the bible **[DECISION]**
- ch4/ch5/ch6/ch7 use **"Sennae Vorlith"**; bible names her only **Vorlith**. Not a contradiction — an addition. Decide: adopt "Sennae" into the bible, or strip to "Vorlith."

### 4. Naming collisions **[HARD]**
- **Roon vs Roan:** the Undersider air-recycler **Roon** (ch4:48, ch5, ch6, ch10:56) is one letter from the Andra Prime syndicate boss **Roan** (major book-two character). Rename one.
- **Maren vs Maren:** bible's **Maren** = Kester's estranged wife / Lira's mother. ch6 (`...ch6.md:28,64,248`) gives "Maren" to an unrelated pediatric nurse. Rename the nurse.
- **Pell vs Pell:** the structural-engineer Undersider **Pell** (dies ch3:44) vs the seven-year-old child **Pell** in the bible/ch6. Confirm intent or rename.

### 5. Bastien's age **[HARD]**
- ch1 (`...the_warden_of_tartaros.md:7`): "He was **sixty**." Bible: born HCE 2320, **age 58** in book one (HCE 2378). Fix to 58.

### 6. Calderon dated "twenty-three years ago" **[HARD]**
- ch11 (`...ch11.md:72`): "The war had been **twenty-three years ago**," and ch11:102 conflates Calderon with Bastien's 23-year program tenure. Bible: **Calderon = HCE 2346, ~32 years before book one.** "Twenty-three years" is the *program/Summum tenure*, not Calderon. Fix Calderon references to ~32 years.
- Related **[BIBLE]**: ch11:74 invents a **16,000-soldier** Calderon scale not in the bible (which only fixes Bastien's 40-man unit, one survivor). If kept, write it back.

### 7. Year stamp clash **[HARD]**
- ch2 (`...ch2.md:170`): record says she "died in **2371**" — book one is **HCE 2378**, and the prefix "HCE" is dropped. Reconcile the year and standardize "HCE."

## Tier 2 — Internal continuity & mechanical errors (objective)

- **ch5 (`:262`):** Lira "ten years old" in 2367 — should be **11** (b. HCE 2356). One-year slip.
- **ch6 (`:104`):** Kester's "dinner table in **2367**" argument predates his **2369** arrival and the chapter's own repeated "nine years." (Same 2367 slip echoes in ch5:262.)
- **ch8 (`:88`):** foreshadow "He would not know it for another **two days**" overshoots the actual ~1-day gap to the Ivo reveal in ch9.
- **ch9 (`:508` vs `:542,556,560`):** Ivo's drop altitude stated as both **300 m** and **400 m**. Pick one. (The cluster of "four hundred" figures — m altitude, km distance, km/h speed — is confusing on a close read.)
- **ch10 (`:54`) vs ch11 (`:168`):** Kester pictures Pira "on the shuttle to Andra Prime" (alive) in ch10, but ch11's procurement aside is framed as him *learning* the shuttle was destroyed. Bible framing implies he carried the secret all along. **Decide:** learns-in-ch11, or already-knew. Book two's false-grief engine depends on it.
- **ch7 (`:522–534`):** "**Nine bodies.**" header immediately followed by three of the four named as *alive* — a momentary false count before the real tally resolves. Staging-clarity pass.
- **Custody→freedom gap:** book one ends with all surviving protagonists in **Harvester custody** (ch11); book two opens two years on with them free and operating. The escape/release is **unwritten** — needs a plan before book two.
- **Broadcast reach:** ch1 (`:119`) "Live. Uncut. **Hegemony-wide**." Bible: the public stays "broadly ignorant," and the Narrine *crushes* Thread B's exposure in book two. Needs an in-world reconciliation (jammed / discredited / contained) so the public ignorance is coherent.

## Tier 3 — Voice & prose polish (the book is strong here; these are the lapses)

- **ch7 (3.5/5):** two back-to-back sermons — **Tev's "eyes open" speech** (`:122–130`) and **Bastien's offer** (`:330–346`) — run long and tip toward stating the theme. Trim / interleave with action beats.
- **Melodrama / theme-announcing (against "slightly cool"):**
  - ch5 (`:376`): Mouse "in some compartment of herself… **screaming**" — spells out the horror the cool surface earned by implication.
  - ch5 (`:346–356`): three stacked italic thesis-declarations ("It was children." / "The prison was a farm." / "yields"). Cut at least one.
  - ch8 (`:252`): Sable's goodbye to Pira piles repetition ("I will remember everything") — her card *refuses* performing her own pain; her tighter promise is "I will remember every day."
  - ch3 (`:184`): narrator explains Mouse's realization on the nose.
- **Repetition tics hardening into crutches:** "He underlined it." ×5 (ch4); "He almost believed himself/it" (ch4:249, ch5:370); anaphoric "The version…" ×4 (ch8:310); "small cold lurch" ×4 (ch10); the name-litany device ×3 in close succession (ch11:344–352); "registered" very dense across ch10–11. Thin these.
- **Davour** reads as a generic "soldier" voice (ch9:512) — bible already flags her as needing a particular war / idiolect. Candidate for a full voice card.
