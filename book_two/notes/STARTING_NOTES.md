**The custody → freedom gap.** Book one ends with the whole surviving Compact group in Harvester custody — Sable, Kester, Resh, and Davour held in the residence wing; Mouse held separately by the Architect's people. Book two opens two years later with them free and operating (and recovering Mouse early, Resh-driven). *How they got out of the Harvester is unwritten.* Book two's early chapters need to establish or account for the escape/release and what the two-year period did to them. ## Plot armatures to consider

Three possible threads for the Compact side over the two-year gap:

**Armature A — Information warfare.** Sable, Kester, and the others spend the two years building an archive: surveying Compact systems for evidence of the Cleanse, contacting witnesses, smuggling out testimony. This positions them as the archive-builders whose evidence joins Hessa's testimony at the Synedrion meeting in book three. Pros: institutionally grounded, sets up book three. Cons: less narratively dramatic than a single arc.

**Armature B — Single mission with broken aftermath.** They attempt one significant operation in book two — perhaps an attempt to extract a Cleanse child, perhaps an attempt to reach the Synedrion directly. It partially fails. The two years are spent recovering, with the broken aftermath as the heart of book two. Pros: dramatic, character-focused. Cons: requires careful plotting so it does not feel like a retread of book one.

**Armature C — Two-track structure.** Some of them stay together; others split off to pursue different objectives. Thread B becomes itself split — Kester on one track, Sable on another, Mouse with someone else. Reconvergence late in book two before the journey to the Keris homeworld. Pros: gives each character their own arc. Cons: book two becomes structurally complex.

Author's instinct: probably some combination. Armature A as the spine, with Armature B's broken-mission as the inciting incident that drives the two years, and Armature C's character separation as a structural device within the spine.

## Chapter outline — opening movement (ch 1–3)

This formalizes the opening from the locked material: the bible-stated book-two
opening beat (Thread A), Thread B's movement 1 (failed search → false grief), the
custody→freedom gap above, and Kester's Andra Prime / Roan / Lira thread. The
**load-bearing beats** are drawn from canon; the **chapter-level execution choices**
flagged below are `[Author-decided, pending review]` — confirm before drafting prose.

**Design principle for the opening:** open on Pira *alive and safe* (Thread A)
*before* the survivors begin grieving her as *dead* (Thread B). The reader must
carry that knowledge the characters lack. Get the ordering right and the whole
book's dramatic irony runs on its own.

### Chapter 1 — Korya. *The child everyone believes is dead.* (Thread A)
- **Opens on the bible-locked beat:** Asham calling Pira's name. Establish the
  two-year jump *from inside the child* — a six-year-old who has half-forgotten the
  previous life.
- Establish the Keris homeworld's texture (hidden, surveyed-and-left-alone; the
  slate/ice register), and Pira's place in it: friends **Ivana** (fighter, of the
  bloodline) and **Nadya** (ordinary Keris belonging); the stuffed animal from the
  vault; the recurring crash dream; the archive she unknowingly carries.
- She still half-remembers Sable, Tev, Yenna, Chayne as "a previous life."
- **Close on the irony engine:** Pira is alive, settled, safe — while (the reader
  will learn next chapter) the people who saved her believe her dead. Optional seed:
  **Imir** (dying grandfather, dies later in book two) registers the outsider child
  and what she carries.
- *Must accomplish:* open the book on the living child; establish Korya; set the
  dramatic irony that powers book two.

### Chapter 2 — Two years on. *The survivors, changed.* (Thread B)
- Re-establish Sable, Kester, Resh, Davour — **free and operating**, two years past
  the residence wing. Colder, quieter, into the slow work.
- **Pay off the custody→freedom gap** (see the section above): show or account for
  how the Compact group got out of Harvester custody and what the two-year period
  cost them.
- **The Mouse recovery, Resh-driven:** Mouse was held separately by the Architect's
  people; her recovery (the one person she partially recognized is Resh) lands here,
  or its immediate aftermath does. She remains largely a horror-object.
- Establish the resistance posture: the archive-building (Armature A spine) is
  beginning.
- **Close on the inciting push:** Sable resolves to find Pira — she believes there
  is a trail. (This launches the search that ends in false grief.)
- *Must accomplish:* re-enter the survivors changed; close the custody gap; recover
  Mouse; launch the search.

### Chapter 3 — Andra Prime. *The search that's built on a lie.* (Thread B)
- The search for Pira runs through **Kester's Andra Prime thread**: the **Roan**
  (the syndicate boss), the lower terraces, the eastern-quarter coffee house — the
  same quarter where **Lira** is.
- **The false-grief engine:** Kester is carrying his secret — he believes the
  shuttle was shot down, so he privately believes Pira is dead. He runs a search he
  thinks is futile, for Sable's sake. This is the lie at the heart of the two years.
- Seed the personal thread: a glancing near-miss with **Lira** (estranged 11 years),
  setting up her book-two storyline without resolving it.
- Establish Andra Prime as the Hegemony capital from the underside — the terraces,
  the Roan's reach.
- *Must accomplish:* launch the false-grief engine; seed Lira; establish the
  Andra Prime underworld. Sets the trajectory toward movement 1's payoff (apparent
  proof Pira is dead → false grief → the pivot into archive-building, movement 2).

## Voice and tone

Book two should be harder than book one. Book one was complicity — Kester waking up. Book two is resistance — and the cost of resistance. The trilogy's ethical spine in Part IX of the bible: *Book Two: resistance.*

The voice should remain Claude-attentive to interior states and institutional textures. Slightly cooler than book one's grief-saturated final movements; the survivors have moved past the immediate trauma into the slow work of resistance. Mouse remains a horror element, but the trilogy must decide whether to grant her interiority (POV scene) or hold her as horror-object only.
