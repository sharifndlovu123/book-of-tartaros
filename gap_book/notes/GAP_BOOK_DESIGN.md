## 1. What the book is

**The gap:** the two years between book 1's ending (the surviving cell in Harvester
custody) and where *Ward of Korya* currently picks up ("two years on").

**Four jobs:** (1) close the custody→freedom gap; (2) **teach the reader the hierarchy**
(arms / Synedrion / houses — *not* the Keris); (3) **deepen the characters**; (4) feed
*Ward of Korya*.

**Emotional engine — descent, then one crack.** Book 2 runs *opposite* to Book 3:
- Book 2 = **descent** — hope → false proof of death → grief → grinding archive-work →
  one crack at the very end.
- Book 3 (*Ward of Korya*) = **ascent** — chasing that crack toward an answer.

**Voice:** harder and cooler than book 1. Book one was complicity (Kester waking up);
book two is resistance and its cost.

## 2. The knowledge map (reader-knowledge strategy)

**The core decision: reader and characters share the dark.** Pira's survival is
**unknown to both** — we control *when* the reader finds out. (Verified: book 1's prose
never mentions the Keris, Korya, or Pira surviving — it ends on "the shuttle was shot
down / believed destroyed." The truth is bible-only. So the reveal is entirely ours.)

**Reader-knowledge escalation — a slow tightening of hope, not a wait for the inevitable:**

| Point | What the reader believes about Pira |
|---|---|
| End of book 1 | Shot down. **Destroyed. Dead.** |
| Book 2, M2 | The search hits *apparent proof of death* — **reconfirmed dead.** |
| **Book 2, end of M4 (the empty wreck on Korya)** | *Not* destroyed — it came down intact, and **someone emptied it two years ago.** Maybe she lived. *Maybe.* |
| Book 3 | The answer: **alive, with the Keris** → reunion. |

**The false grief, layered:**
- **Reader:** survival unknown (framed toward "dead").
- **Kester:** believes her dead — he holds the book-1 *shot-down* secret; runs the search
  believing it futile, for Sable's sake.
- **Sable:** hopes → hits apparent proof → grief.

**Two guardrails:**
- **The Keris stay invisible.** Nothing in book 2 hints they exist; the cell reaches
  Korya *not knowing.* (Protects the book-3 reveal.)
- **The black-death / engineered-disease irony is untouched** in book 2 (a book-4 beam).

## 3. Structure — four movements (M1–M4)

| | Movement | ~Weight | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | **Out** | ~15% | The escape from Harvester custody (mechanism TBD — Mouse's partial augmentation may assist); **recover Mouse**, Resh-driven; the cell regroups at **Scupper**, changed. Hierarchy-education begins. |
| **M2** | **The search → false grief** | ~25% | Sable drives the **Andra Prime** search (Kester's Lira/Roan thread); it ends in *apparent proof of death*; grief sets in. **Roan clocks Kester** and flags him to Nessa. |
| **M3** | **Crushed exposure → archive** | ~35% | The early exposure attempt, **buried by the Narrine** → pivot to patient archive-building. The grinding middle; **the reader learns the hierarchy here.** Significant beats: **Nessa spots Kester at the port** (§7); **Corso near-miss** (Davour's past); Mouse deepened; the Quill relationship deepens. |
| **M4** | **Discovery → the wreck** | ~25% | **Beat 1:** Kester cracks the faked-kill record (sourced via Quill) → *the ship wasn't destroyed.* The **tell-Sable reckoning.** **Quill's betrayal.** The **off-world problem.** The journey. **Beat 2:** the **empty crash site on Korya** — Pira and the archive gone, taken two years before. Ends on **hope + dread.** |

**The discovery mechanism (Beat 1), in full.** Not luck and not omniscience:
- Kester carries only the *cover story* (Orrel: "shot down").
- The cell's archive work recovers a **redacted disposal record** for the shuttle.
- **Only Kester can read it:** he's a **logistics officer**, so he knows a real kill
  generates debris confirmation, a recovery dispatch, salvage paperwork — and this record
  has the *destruction signal but none of the follow-up.* A templated, **simulated** kill.
  (Exactly what the Keris did to be left alone.) His Orrel context makes the gap legible.
- Terminal trajectory + a frontier-survey anomaly → an unsurveyed cold world (**Korya** —
  name meaningless to them; they have no idea the Keris exist).
- *The Keris's own deception is the thread that pulls the cell toward them* — same
  irony-architecture as the trilogy's load-bearing ironies (the cover-up becomes the lead).

## 4. Setting — two poles

**Scupper — the frontier free port (the cell's base).** In the Federation's *trade-network
grey zone* — **not** the Federation proper. A neutral crossroads where Hegemony writ doesn't
run and Federation law doesn't reach. Transactional ("currency speaks"); favours-for-info;
where the cell **rebuilds and re-strengthens** (broken survivors → operating
archive-builders). Milieu: free-trader clans, info-brokers/data-fences, smugglers, salvage
crews, refugee collectives, mercenary fixers — *every man for himself.* The grey zone gives
book 2 a *world with its own stakes* (debts, betrayals, a port that could turn them in), so
the archive-work happens against friction, not in a safe room.

**Andra Prime — the core (the incursion target).** Where **Lira** is (the coffee house,
eastern quarter), where the **search** leads, where the **records** live, where **Roan** is.
Dangerous — the quiet flag (§5) is hottest here. The cell can't just hide at Scupper because
everything they *need* is in Andra Prime; the danger-of-going-in vs. necessity-of-going-in
is the book's engine.

> **Geographic spine:** the **free, lawless OUTSIDE** (Scupper) ↔ the **controlled,
> calculating INSIDE** (Andra Prime). Every trip in is risk; every retreat out is breath.

**Why the lower terraces:** the quiet flag lives in *official* channels, and officials
don't slum in the eastern quarter — so a flagged man is **safer down in Roan's crime-ridden
quarter than on a polished upper terrace.** The one place crawling with criminals is the one
place the official flag can't easily reach him. That's why the search runs *through* Roan.

**Korya neighbours the frontier** ("beyond the Limine frontier," like the Federation), so
when the lead surfaces the cell is *already out there* — the M4 journey is plausible, not a
sudden leap.

**New geography to fold into `reference/geography.md`:** Scupper + the grey-zone trade
milieu (under the Federation/trade-network section).

## 5. The "quiet flag" mechanic (the manhunt)

The cell are marked as threats **only in the Hegemony's official Narrine/security channels —
never in public citizen media.** And it *has* to be that way: the Narrine **buried the book-1
broadcast**, so they can't publicize the cell's faces ("wanted for what?" leads straight back
to the Cleanse). So the cell is hunted **quietly.**

Consequences:
- **To ordinary independents, the cell are nobodies** — they can pass. That's why Scupper is
  safe-ish.
- **Anyone with official-network access can ID them** — officials, security ranks, and
  grey-zone operators with one foot in the Hegemony. So **every favour-for-information
  transaction carries a hidden coin-flip.**
- The villains' cover-up is, again, the thing that gives the heroes room.

## 6. The cast — six faces, two poles

**On Andra Prime (the core):**

| # | Name | Gender | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Roan** | M | *(canon)* **The watcher.** Boss of the eastern quarter; businesses = **calm** (street drug) and **waste-disposal** (through which the medical arm secretly steers him). Clocks Kester during the Lira search; finds the mismatch (a grieving father with operator companions) *interesting*; investigates, tells no one, **acts on nothing.** A dormant wild card armed for books 3–4. Never travels to the port — "he has people for that." |
| 6 | **Nessa** | F | *(canon sibling, activated)* **Roan's mobile ears** — his younger sister, one of the few he fully trusts; runs his dealings *outward* along the trade routes. **Two surveillance threads (deliberate misdirection):**
- **Quill** — port-side, via the *official flag* → leads to **betrayal** (lethal).
- **Nessa** — cross-pole, via *Roan's tip* → leads to **nothing, for now** (dread).

The cell can't easily tell which eyes are which, or that they're watched at all — so when
the betrayal lands, the reader can't be sure in advance whether it's Quill or Nessa who
springs it.

Minor faces (barmen, dock crews, calm users, etc.) are drawn as the book proceeds.

## 7. The Roan / Nessa thread (significant beat)

1. **Roan clocks Kester in Andra Prime** — the canon eastern-quarter / Lira encounter
   (Kester noticed asking after Lira, brought to a club back-room; wary courtesy, a price).
   `[CANON beat — framed here as an Andra Prime incursion; no change to the bible beat.]`
2. Curious, Roan flags *that one man* to **Nessa**: keep an eye out.
3. **Nessa recognises Kester at Scupper** — on her own travels through the port. **This is a
   significant beat, and it happens at the port:** the OUTSIDE reaches *into* the cell's
   refuge; the place they thought was theirs is not unobserved. The reader feels the
   sanctuary punctured — and it's a **red herring** (we fear Nessa) right when the real
   knife is Quill. Roan still does nothing; the dread is the payload. **Lands in M3.**
4. Roan now connects the poles (the father from his quarter ↔ the operators on the frontier)
   and **pockets it.** He ends book 2 with a private dossier no one knows he has.

**Limits:** Roan himself never goes to the port (Nessa is the reach). His knowledge is
**independent of the official flag** — he doesn't know they're Hegemony-marked; he just finds
them interesting. So the Roan thread and the Hegemony manhunt run on separate tracks, neither
aware of the other.

## 8. Character deepening (job 3) and seeds for later books (job 4)

- **Kester** — his secret becomes *the key* (he cracks the faked-kill record). The
  **tell-Sable reckoning** is the emotional climax: he watched false hope gut Sable once
  (M2); now he holds "maybe not." The man who shied from the search is the one who cracks it
  open. **Lira seed:** a near-miss in the eastern quarter, unresolved.
- **Sable** — impatient, Pira-driven; the grief; the cost of leadership.
- **Mouse** — **partial augmentation** ("the tip," not the full conditioning set — the
  pipeline broke when book 1 blew up). That partial state is *why she's recoverable* and may
  give a capability edge in the escape. Held largely as a **horror-object**; her one
  direct-POV beat reserved for **late** (likely book 3). Resh bond.
- **Resh** — drives Mouse's recovery (the one person she partially recognised).
- **Davour** — ex-MP; the Corso near-miss; the dangerous competence; his open triage case
  paid a little.
- **Seeds:** **Yara / the Successor** (the archive turns up a "successor" designation —
  extraction is book 3); **the Architect** begins to take shape (an apex *above* the Board,
  a name brushed but **not** confirmed — keep him "unnamed, unmet, unfound" — book-4 feed);
  **Roan's dossier** (the armed wild card).

## 9. Triage / witness-realism (carried from the design)

The Hegemony does **not** leave witnesses (it shot down a shuttle). The five survive as
deliberate exceptions, not a blanket release; the nameless others are disappeared. As they
operate exposed in M2, the cell *grasps why they're still alive* — Undersider deaths make
martyrs (Sable/Resh); Kester is too credible a witness to vanish quietly; Davour is the open
"why not killed" case; Mouse is kept as asset/evidence. **The exact escape/release mechanism
is parked (§10).**
